# THRIVE Living Signal Environment Standard v0.1

Date: 2026-10-06  
Status: **APPROVED CURRENT VISUAL STANDARD**  
Owner: DSS Enterprises  
Applies to: participant-facing Today, Wellness, Goals, Money, Support, Resources, Recovery Support, and Story  
Production impact: none by this document  
Database impact: none  
Trust Engine impact: none

## Purpose

This document preserves the approved THRIVE visual experience standard so future threads, branches, and implementation passes do not drift back toward old THRIVE styling or treat Living Signal as a cosmetic skin.

It consolidates the approved visual direction from:

- `docs/THRIVE_EXPERIENCE_RESET_ROADMAP_v1_0.md`
- `docs/THRIVE_VISUAL_EXPERIENCE_DIRECTION_v0_1.md`
- the accepted Living Signal Today implementation
- the accepted Wellness implementation
- the approved THRIVE visual direction boards reviewed during the September 30 to October 6 experience-reset work

Today and Wellness are the current implementation benchmarks for environmental quality. They are not templates to copy literally. They prove the level of visual life, atmosphere, depth, and lane-specific identity THRIVE is expected to maintain.

## North star

THRIVE is a **responsive support environment**, not a tracker with nicer cards.

The participant experience should feel:

> **Calm enough to trust, alive enough to return to, direct enough to help.**

The participant should be able to **feel that something changed before reading a paragraph explaining it**.

Visual design has equal standing with functional usefulness. It is not a finishing pass added after the workflow is complete.

## Core Living Signal grammar

Every participant-facing lane should express these four Living Signal properties:

1. **Atmosphere**  
   The page has a contextual visual mood and sense of place.

2. **Signal**  
   Important participant state is visually obvious before deep reading.

3. **Motion**  
   Meaningful state changes may move, arrive, advance, resolve, carry, open, or acknowledge.

4. **Accumulation**  
   Effort, progress, completion, and carry-forward remain visible instead of disappearing.

A page that merely adds a gradient, glass card, scenic background, or new palette to the old card stack does **not** meet the Living Signal standard.

## Environment is part of the interface

The environment must participate in the meaning of the page.

The target is not:

```text
background image
+ glass card
+ old workflow
```

The target is:

```text
lane meaning
+ environmental metaphor
+ participant state
+ visual hierarchy
+ meaningful movement
+ useful action
```

The environment should shape how the first viewport feels, not simply sit behind content.

## Lane-specific environmental metaphor

THRIVE uses one shared design system with **distinct lane environments**.

The exact image does not need to be the same every time. A lane may use a small approved family of scenes, illustrations, atmospheric variants, or daypart states.

The rule is:

> **Shared THRIVE visual DNA, distinct lane environment, variable scene within that lane's metaphor.**

A lane environment should reinforce what that lane means.

### Today

Role: home, orientation, return, current direction.

Environmental language may include:

- sunrise / fresh-start scenes;
- evening sanctuary scenes;
- horizon, path, light, weather, open view;
- daypart-responsive atmosphere;
- layered depth that feels like a living home surface.

Today may borrow visual signals from other lanes because it is the participant's home environment.

### Wellness

Role: reflection, check-in, awareness, internal state, return.

Environmental language may include:

- light, water, sky, breath, calm weather, reflection;
- softer spatial depth;
- daypart variation;
- room for quiet focus;
- responsive state without implying diagnosis or emotional inference.

Wellness should feel human and reflective, not clinical.

### Goals

Role: movement, direction, distance, checkpoints, possibility, progress.

Environmental language may include:

- ridge paths;
- winding roads;
- overlooks;
- lake trails;
- mountain routes;
- sunrise/sunset routes;
- visible destination or checkpoint language;
- path/progress geography.

The image may vary. The metaphor should still feel like **movement toward something**.

Goals must not collapse into static scenic wallpaper plus cards.

### Money

Role: flow, cycle, stability, stewardship, movement, carrying forward.

Environmental language may include:

- flow, current, season, cycle, horizon, grounded landscape, moving light;
- visual rhythm that can close, settle, reset, or carry into a next cycle;
- richer material depth without becoming a bank dashboard;
- completion imagery that makes a closed Money cycle feel materially different from ordinary activity.

Money must remain alive, warm, and human. "Practical" does not mean sterile, conservative, institutional, spreadsheet-like, or corporate-finance-like.

### Support

Role: connection, warmth, refuge, access, human help, pathways outward.

Environmental language may include:

- warmth;
- connected paths;
- welcoming light;
- places of refuge;
- bridge / doorway / gathering metaphors;
- visible continuity between asking, waiting, replying, and resolving.

Support should feel available and human, not like escalation workflow or a ticketing system.

### Resources / Recovery Support

Role: real places, real options, practical next steps, connection to the outside world.

Environmental language may include:

- place, route, locality, access, movement outward;
- real-world action surfaces;
- location-aware or resource-aware visual context when authorized;
- a sense that the participant is moving from reflection into the world.

Resource cards should feel actionable, not like generic directories.

### Story

Role: accumulation, memory, continuity, chapters, carry-forward.

Environmental language may include:

- progress geography;
- path segments;
- layered timeline;
- chapters;
- accumulated markers;
- changing terrain;
- multi-lane visual identity.

Story should not look like an ordinary feed.

## Scene variation is allowed and encouraged

The same lane does not need one permanent image.

Approved direction:

- multiple scene variants per lane;
- morning / afternoon / evening variants where useful;
- state-responsive composition where grounded in real participant state;
- completion or carry-forward variants;
- seasonal or atmospheric variation where it does not imply unsupported emotional conclusions.

Variation must preserve lane identity.

Changing scenery is not a license to change the product meaning.

## First viewport standard

The first mobile viewport should feel like an **experience**, not a stack of equally weighted cards.

It should answer, visually and functionally:

- Where am I?
- What kind of place is this lane?
- What matters now?
- What changed?
- What can I do next?

The first viewport should use hierarchy, negative space, environmental depth, typography, and state to create a clear focal point.

## Typography as emotion

Typography is part of the environmental system.

Use type hierarchy to create:

- calm;
- urgency without alarm;
- accomplishment;
- direction;
- reflection;
- continuation.

Do not flatten every lane into the same heavy dashboard sans-serif rhythm.

Display typography should support the lane's emotional function while remaining readable and adult.

## Layered depth

Approved ingredients include:

- environmental illustration or imagery;
- soft translucent layering;
- subtle texture or grain;
- organic curves;
- controlled shadows;
- foreground / middle / background relationships;
- atmospheric depth;
- readable surfaces that feel embedded in the scene.

Avoid using glassmorphism as a substitute for hierarchy.

## Motion grammar

Motion must mean something.

Approved motion states:

- **arrive**: a useful return enters;
- **advance**: progress visibly moves;
- **resolve**: completion settles;
- **carry**: unfinished work bridges forward;
- **open**: detail expands;
- **acknowledge**: a meaningful milestone receives a brief visual response.

Avoid:

- constant decorative looping motion;
- confetti for routine actions;
- punitive shaking;
- flashing failure states;
- animation that competes with reading;
- motion that exists only to make the app feel busy.

Ambient motion may be used lightly when it contributes to atmosphere and does not obscure the participant's task.

## Completion must transform, not disappear

Meaningful completion should produce a visible artifact or changed environmental state.

Examples:

### Goal
- checkpoint reached;
- path advances;
- completion moment;
- visible artifact in Story;
- build-on choice.

### Money
- cycle closes;
- totals settle;
- environment or progress geometry resolves;
- grounded closeout return;
- optional carry-forward.

### Wellness
- cumulative participation rhythm becomes visible;
- no brittle streak punishment.

### Support
- continuing thread changes from asking to waiting to resolving;
- resolved help remains part of Story.

## Partial completion and unfinished work

Partial completion is not failure.

The visual system should support:

- started;
- moving;
- paused;
- resumed;
- partially completed;
- completed;
- carried forward.

Possible visual metaphors:

- path stops before the next marker;
- checkpoint remains visible;
- arc partially fills;
- unfinished segment carries forward.

No shame, failure theater, or punitive visual treatment.

## Lane identity

All lanes share THRIVE branding, spacing logic, accessibility intent, mobile navigation, touch-target philosophy, and general visual quality.

They should not all share the same atmosphere.

The system should avoid:

- hospital-green domination;
- rainbow chaos;
- one universal scenic image;
- one universal card composition;
- one universal hero treatment.

Lane identity may use distinct accent families, icon language, environment, visual metaphor, and completion treatment.

## Non-negotiable acceptance rubric

A participant-facing lane is not visually complete until the answer is "yes" to these questions:

1. Does the environment participate in meaning?
2. Can the participant feel the lane before reading it?
3. Does meaningful state visibly alter the composition?
4. Is the first viewport an experience rather than a card stack?
5. Does the lane have its own atmosphere while clearly remaining THRIVE?
6. Does meaningful progress leave a visible artifact, movement, or changed state?
7. Would the participant have a reason to want to open the page again even if they already know the raw data?
8. Does the lane feel materially less institutional and materially more alive?
9. Is the visual treatment adult, warm, direct, and usable on a phone?
10. Does the page avoid becoming a decorative skin over the old interaction model?

If several answers are "no," do not polish locally and freeze. Reconcile the environment first.

## Thread re-anchor rule

When a new THRIVE implementation thread starts, review this document before visually changing Today, Wellness, Goals, Money, Support, Resources, Recovery Support, or Story.

If a proposed implementation conflicts with this standard, stop and say:

> Alignment check: this appears to be moving beyond the approved gate.

Then reconcile the proposal against this document before continuing.

## Frozen boundaries

This visual standard does not authorize:

- schema changes;
- Supabase writes;
- Trust Engine synchronization;
- bank-data intent inference;
- clinical interpretation;
- historical rewrites;
- fabricated participant state;
- fabricated completion;
- production deployment;
- route replacement;
- destructive cleanup.

The database remains truth.

Visual treatment may respond only to supported facts and approved contextual inputs such as time of day.
